4 — Stair section

Cut through stringer B. Each stringer runs 9 under the landing: its top plumb cut, at y 18, is 7.01 tall — close to the 2×8 rim it bears on. Stringers are dropped ¾ for the ¾ plywood treads — no nosing, just a 1/8 overhang with a tape-sealed edge, painted. Tread/riser joint: the riser board goes on the front of each plumb cut, its bottom edge on the stringer’s horizontal cut for the tread below; that tread starts where the riser ends and butts its face. Simple butt joint, riser behind the tread, nothing notched — the nosing-to-nosing going is exactly 9. Rev AE: stringer A moved ¾ out onto the half-wall framing at 106¼ — the ¾ ply that used to sit between them, and behind the whole landing box, is gone, and what is left of the half-wall’s stair face is a SKIRT: hw_sheath_stair, one board cut to the stringers’ own top line and lapping nothing. Boards are 24 uphill of the wall’s end and 25½ past it, where they lap the skin on stringer A’s outer face; board 3 straddles the end cap at y 50¾ and is notched 1½ × 5. At the foot the stringers are notched 1½ × 3½ over the kicker. 5 — Under-stair nook

Rev AE. The face is one piece and the tube is behind it. The nook face — everything you see at x 102, floor to deck — was three ply panels with the ends of all three lining sheets showing between them: six edges to fill. It is now two boards: a 4¼ stile in the bed-wall corner, and one 45¾ × 53¾ board with the opening sawn out of it, running unbroken round the head and out to the corner you walk past. One seam, and it falls on y 4¼ — the same line as the opening jamb below it. It is two and not one only because the face is 50 × 53¾ and neither dimension fits a 48 sheet; a 5×5 panel would make it one. The lining is recessed ¾ behind it — nk_wrap_bedwall, nk_wrap_shortwall and the soffit panels all stop at x 102¾ instead of running out to the face, so the face laps their ends and no end grain shows on it. Their faces are unmoved, so the finished opening is the 41½ × 40¾ it was; the face panel's own edge is the first ¾ of each jamb and the lining takes over behind it. The tube itself is ¾ plywood from x 102¾ straight through to the right wall — no jambs, no separate head trim. Its ceiling is flat at 40¾ to y = 18.26, where the stringer undersides come down to that line, then rides them to 14.86 at the far end. Rev AE: that ceiling is two boards, not one — nk_soffit_flat and nk_soffit_rake. It was drawn and encoded as a single sheet with a 42.5° kink along it, which is not a thing you can cut; the two now butt on a plumb joint at y 18.26, where both faces are on 40¾, so the joint is flush and full-thickness and the ceiling still reads as one plane turning a corner. the framing plane above it is 41½, the bottom of both the nook header and the landing ledgers, so the panel screws straight to them with nothing to fur. Inside the wall the raked run is carried by hw_rake_nailer, a 2×4 laid flat with its underside on the stringer line, bevelled where it dies into the header. The two sides are ordinary 2×4 nailer walls — studs on edge, 1½ × 3½, on a 2×4 laid flat and run right up to the bed wall. They are a full 3½ deep because Rev Z added a ½ ply flitch inside each jamb pack (king + ½ ply + trimmer = 3½, the same lay-up as the header), so the trimmer faces and the nook stud faces are one plane and the ¾ wrap crosses the reveal without a step. On the short wall the three struts land at x 106¼, 117 7/8 and 129½ — directly under stringers A, B and C. Rev AE: both plates now run through the half wall — hw_bottom_plate_a and hw_bottom_plate_b are each one 28¼ board carrying the half-wall's jamb pack and then the nook's studs, because the ¾ sheathing that used to separate them at x 106¼→107 is gone.
