3 — Section through the platform edge

Cut at x = 55, between joists, so the joist reads pale (beyond) and the ply, ledger, beam and top plate are hatched (cut). The deck ply is the floor of the ledge well. The rear ledger is the same depth as the joists — top and bottom both flush, top at 58 so the ply runs straight over it to the bed wall, framing bottom 53¾, same plane as the beam's own underside. Rev AP: a ½ finished ceiling now closes that plane from below, 53¼ finished — the same 53¼ clear height under the joists and the beam, from the bed wall out to 50½. Rev AG: the beam is a single 2×14 LVL wrapped on three sides — face, bed side and a cap the slats stand on — and its finished top at 68¼ stands 2¼ proud of the 8 mattress, so it retains it rather than sitting below it the way Rev T's 63 beam did. The boxed ledge tracks it: a 10¼ rail keeps the lid 3 proud of the mattress, the curb Rev L chose. Sitting headroom is 30 over a measured 8 mattress. Rev AU/AX: the cut at x = 55 falls in the bay between joists 4 and 5, so it passes through two different blocks, both hatched. On edge at the beam face (9 of them, one per bay) is the diaphragm's fixing to the beam. Flat at y 24 (5 of them, only where the ply is seamed) backs the deck's cross-joist seam — the ply really is two panels butting on it at this station, which is why the joint line is drawn. ledge_rail_splice is cut here too. The joists hang off the beam, so without them the deck ply's front edge lands at the LVL with nothing under it and nothing fastening it: the diaphragm that stands in for posts had no connection to the beam at all. Rev AN: the slats are centred on the beam rather than flush with its face — a grille 5/8 back on both sides, not one plane with the wrap.

6 — Platform framing plan

Joists hang off the rear ledger and off the beam's inside face (LUS24 hangers, both ends — they cannot 'land on' a beam that shares their bottom). Nothing bears in the field; the platform is carried by the two walls and the half-wall. Lay the joists out so plywood seams land on centres. Rev AG: the window end is a different joint. The side ledger is a 2×4 at deck level, so nothing of it shows above the ply and the ledge's front rail needs no notch — but a 2×4 cannot back the beam's end, so the beam does not butt it. Joist 0 runs on past the beam face to y 50, sistered to the ledger over its whole 48½, and the beam is notched 3 × 3½ out of its bottom left corner and bears on the pair — 3 × 1¾ = 5.25 sq in, ~115 psi against 425 allowable, where before it was end grain against a hanger nobody had chosen. The other eight joists stop at the beam face on LUS24s. Two things follow for the builder: the ledger's screws want a stud within 6 of y 50 (or blocking added — the reaction lands 1½–3 off the wall face and a 3½-deep ledger has a short couple to resist it), and the deck ply must be glued and screwed down onto the ledger, because that diaphragm is what stops it rolling.

7 — Half-wall detail

Plan detail is a horizontal section at 12 — deliberately low, because the opening head is raked and only below 15.11 is the opening its full 43 rough width across the whole span. Rotated so the bed wall is on the left and the stair side up. The opening removes both faces: what remains is two posts and a sandwich header — a portal frame, not a shear wall. The beam's reaction bypasses it (lands on the far stud pack); the landing box ties the top plate across to the right wall. Glue the deck ply. The header-to-king joints are NOT strapped (Rev AS): the wall is sheathed across both of them — hw_sheath_stair on the stair face and the one-piece nook face on the loft face — and the two continuous top plates bear over the header and both kings. Rev AZ took the loft face to ½ and the builder accepted it: the joint carries gravity into the trimmers, whose bearing is checked, so the sheathing is a racking and continuity gusset rather than a tension tie. Rev AE: the stair side of the wall is not sheathed below the stair. Stringer A lands on the framing at x 106¼, the landing box with it, and both bottom plates run on through into the nook as single boards; what is left of the stair face is hw_sheath_stair, a skirt above the stringers’ top line. At this cut the wall is therefore 4 thick, not 5.
