4 — Stair section

Shaded areas are beyond the section plane — the half-wall, the loft deck, the boxed ledge, and the beam end. All seven risers are a uniform 8¼", including the last step from landing to deck. Handrail on the right wall for the full run; loft side guarded by the half-wall where the drop exceeds 30", finished skirt below that. Note the headroom dimensions: above about 38" of tread height you're ducking, so the top two steps and the landing are a crouch for everyone.

5 — Under-stair nook

Ceiling height is set by the nook header (doubled 2×10, bottom at 41½") — that member is sized by what the landing rim needs to hang from, not by load, so the opening head and the flat ceiling line up at the same height. The gussets over the 18½"–27" transition carry the soffit panel only; the stair's load runs through the rim into the header, never through them. Power has to be run into the half-wall before it's sheathed; switch on the half-wall face, reachable from the desk side.

6 — Platform framing plan

Joists run front-to-back, hung off the rear ledger and landing on the beam. Nothing bears in the field — the whole platform is carried by the two walls and the half-wall. Lay the joists out so plywood seams land on centres; field-adjust spacing rather than accepting an unsupported seam.

7 — Half-wall detail

The opening removes both faces. What's left is two short posts joined by a header — a portal frame, not a shear wall. The beam's reaction bypasses it entirely (lands on the far doubled stud), and the landing framing ties the top plate across to the right wall, so vertical load and top-of-wall bracing are both fine. But the deck diaphragm is now doing all the lateral work at this end: glue the plywood, and strap both header-to-post joints. Deck rim still set inboard to 106¼".

8 — Stair & landing framing

The header carries everything on the half-wall side. There is no wall to land a ledger on — the opening runs 3"–47" and the landing sits 0"–27" — so the landing joists and the rim all hang off the header's stair-side face. The landing is framed as a box, not hung off a single member. A ¾" ply facing on the header's stair face brings it flush with the wall plane, so everything downstream references one line. Then: 2×4 ledgers on the bed wall and right wall, a 2×8 side member screwed to the flush header with ¼"×5" structural screws through both plies, and a 2×12 rim running the full 24" wall to wall with its left end hangered to the header. Joists span between the side member and the right-wall ledger. Two consequences worth knowing: the rim runs through rather than butting the side member, which keeps a continuous face for all three stringers and holds the stair at 24"; and the side member is a 2×8 rather than a 2×10 so its underside tucks just above the nook's 41½" ceiling instead of hanging 1¾" below it. Load path, end to end: stringers → 2×10 rim → nook header at one end and a right-wall ledger at the other → trimmers → bottom plate → floor. Nothing structural passes through the soffit gussets; they carry a panel. The rim is a 2×12 because the stringer is 15¼" deep where it lands, giving 11¼" of face to fasten to, and its bottom still sits above the nook soffit where it stays hidden. Its left end hangs off the header with a joist hanger — the half-wall is open there, so it cannot bear on the wall. Throat comes out at 5.15" against a 3½" minimum; lay out and cut one stringer first, measure the actual throat, then cut the other two.

9 — Framing overlay · half-wall + stair

On the half-wall side the joists land on the 2×8 side member and the rim hangers directly to the flush-faced header, so the landing has two independent paths into the wall rather than one. The stair itself is held by the rim at the top, the kicker at the bottom, stringer C's tie to the right wall, and stringer A's contact with the end stud at ➍. Cross-check this against Drawings 7 and 8 before framing: if any member's height range here disagrees with those, this drawing is the one to trust, since it's the only view where both assemblies are drawn to the same datum.

Ceiling joists run parallel to the bed wall — the top plate can't cross them. Resolve in this order: (1) measure the joist line nearest 50¾" out — the plate spans 47¼"–50¾", so anything landing in that band works as drawn; if it falls outside, adjust the ledge (6"–10" all work) to move the plate onto it; (2) block between joists from the attic; (3) block from below through a 4" drywall strip. Don't skip it — unattached, a 200 lb lean at the top of the screen puts ~1,950 psi into 2×2 baluster bases against ~900 allowable.
